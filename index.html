What Shaped My Approach

A lot of how I think about customers, attention and messaging started long before I became a copywriter.

I started in nonprofit fundraising, talking to donors one-to-one before helping run nationwide street campaigns.

That work grew donations by 10–20% a year and cut donor attrition by 22%.

Later, I built my own fashion brand.

We showed at London Fashion Week, sold B2B and direct to customers, worked with artists and creators, and became one of the founding brands in ASOS’s first sustainable fashion concept.

I also built an email list of around 5,000 people...

...and barely used it.

That one still makes me laugh.

Because having people’s attention and knowing what to do with it are two very different things.

These days, that experience sits underneath how I work.

I’m interested in what a brand wants to say, what people care about, and where the two overlap.

How I Work

I keep the process as simple as the project allows.

My defined offers are completely call-free.

You send me what I need. I research, think, write, and send the work back in a Google Doc.

From there, we refine it together without turning every decision into another meeting.

For larger or more involved projects, we’ll start with a proper conversation to work out the scope and make sure we’re solving the right problem.

After that, communication can be as hands-on as the project needs.

Calls when they help. Async when they don’t.

No discovery-call obstacle course. No meetings just to prove the project is moving.
